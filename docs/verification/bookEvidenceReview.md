# Evidence review of the remaining book issues

Updated 2026-11-03. All **30 entries** in the source-checked editorial list were reviewed: **9 DSM and 21 Kaplan**. **28 entries** have external research/official-source evidence or a stated qualification; **2 are source-only contradictions** that outside literature cannot settle. Every entry now has an explicit dated note in **both its Arabic and English explanation**, with reference links where applicable. This is a bounded review of selected claims, not a complete fact-check of all 1,147 imported rows.

Research used PubMed and public primary/official sources. OpenEvidence and VeraHealth were not used. Some papers were accessible only as abstracts or indexed passages; those limits are listed below and in the machine-readable records. No translation engine or book-PDF upload was used.

The source PDFs and raw extracted English are preserved. Original keys, options, question/case identities and relationships are unchanged. **A corrected explanation does not make an ambiguous or clinically unsupported printed key suitable for automatic grading.** Such items still need a decision to rewrite the stem/options/key together or exclude them from grading. Incidental wording corrections and conditional answers do not automatically invalidate the original key.

## Per-question findings

### DSM-5

**1.5** — Intellectual disability requires deficits in both intellectual and adaptive functioning, with developmental onset. IQ alone does not establish the diagnosis. Severity is based on adaptive functioning and support needs, rather than an IQ band. Thus the statement allowing diagnosis from a low IQ without adaptive deficits is also false, as is keyed E. The printed item does not establish a unique false answer. [www.psychiatry.org](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/intellectual-disability/expert-q-and-a)

**Disposition:** Printed key retained; multiple false choices. **Access:** Accessible primary/official page or PubMed abstract; not a claim of full-text access.

**1.43** — The restlessness, impulsivity and inattention suggest ADHD, and the chronic irritability raises a DMDD differential. However, DMDD should not first be diagnosed before age six. The printed patient is five, so keyed ADHD plus DMDD cannot be accepted as a fully established diagnosis at the stated age. Do not silently change the age or infer that irritability alone proves a different disorder. [www.nimh.nih.gov](https://www.nimh.nih.gov/health/publications/disruptive-mood-dysregulation-disorder)

**Disposition:** Printed age/key conflict retained and explained. **Access:** Accessible primary/official page or PubMed abstract; not a claim of full-text access.

**2.5** — DSM-5 schizoaffective disorder requires major mood episodes during the majority of the total active and residual illness, as well as a period of psychosis without a major mood episode. The printed sequence gives two months of psychosis, three months with depression and psychosis, then one month of psychosis: the mood episode occupies three of six months, not a majority. Psychosis outside depression alone does not establish the keyed diagnosis. The vignette does not justify inventing additional mood duration or a definitive replacement diagnosis. [PubMed 23707642](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23707642/)

**Disposition:** Printed key not established by stated timeline. **Access:** Accessible primary/official page or PubMed abstract; not a claim of full-text access.

**16.9** — The proposed ND-PAE exposure criterion is more than 13 drinks per month OR more than two drinks on an occasion during pregnancy. It describes more-than-minimal exposure for a proposed diagnostic construct; it is not a safe drinking allowance. The question asks about more-than-minimal exposure, but its choices describe lower exposure. Keyed E is not the stated greater-than threshold. No safe amount of alcohol in pregnancy has been established. [PubMed 27677572](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27677572/), [www.cdc.gov](https://www.cdc.gov/fasd/about/)

**Disposition:** Printed threshold/choice direction conflict. **Access:** Accessible primary/official page or PubMed abstract; not a claim of full-text access.

**22.10** — The LPFS grades impairment from 0 to 4. In the alternative personality-disorder model, at least moderate impairment (level 2) is required; the formal level called some impairment is level 1 and is insufficient. Keyed A is therefore false if it means that formal level, while E is also problematic if it denies graded impairment. If some is merely ordinary wording for any impairment, A reads differently. The item needs editorial clarification rather than an asserted unique key. [PubMed 23995027](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23995027/)

**Disposition:** Conditional wording and overlapping false-choice concern. **Access:** Accessible primary/official page or PubMed abstract; not a claim of full-text access.

**1.32** — The pattern of inattention and executive difficulties supports the intended predominantly inattentive ADHD answer. However, the WAIS-IV normative age range starts at 16 years (through 90 years 11 months); the printed patient is 15. The stated use of that instrument is an age/norm mismatch. The record does not authorize changing her age, substituting another instrument, or fabricating scores. A cognitive score alone is not a diagnostic test for ADHD. [www.pearsonclinical.asia](https://www.pearsonclinical.asia/content/dam/school/global/clinical/us/assets/wais-iv/presentation-introducing-the-wais-iv.pdf)

**Disposition:** Incidental instrument-age correction; key retained. **Access:** Accessible primary/official page or PubMed abstract; not a claim of full-text access.

**4.29** — Premenstrual syndrome does not require the five-symptom threshold and core affective symptom pattern required for PMDD. The intended PMS answer is retained. Separately, the verified drospirenone/ethinyl estradiol product is an oral tablet, whereas the vignette calls it a patch. This is a formulation mismatch in the source; do not substitute an unidentified patch or infer an unreported treatment response. This check does not survey every product worldwide. [dailymed.nlm.nih.gov](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6d85f252-307c-4aa8-9572-1e74c9819283)

**Disposition:** Formulation qualification; diagnostic key retained. **Access:** Accessible primary/official page or PubMed abstract; not a claim of full-text access.

**16.13** — Intravenous and intramuscular administration are parenteral routes. Choice A pairs nonparenteral with IV/IM examples and therefore contains an internal terminology contradiction; it remains an incorrect distractor and should not be silently repaired into a correct answer. The source selects B for the exclusion of the listed nonbenzodiazepine anxiolytics from this sedative-use-disorder grouping. Treatment of anxiety alone does not put every medicine in that substance class. [www.fda.gov](https://www.fda.gov/media/169646/download?attachment=)

**Disposition:** Terminology clarified; incorrect distractor and key retained. **Access:** Accessible primary/official page or PubMed abstract; not a claim of full-text access.

**17.12** — Huntington disease is autosomal dominant. HTT alleles with 36–39 CAG repeats have reduced penetrance; alleles with at least 40 repeats are considered fully penetrant given a normal lifespan. Thus the original claim that every allele with at least 36 repeats invariably causes disease is too broad. C captures the intended inheritance pattern, but its blanket complete-penetrance wording requires this qualification; none of the other printed choices supplies the complete correction. [PubMed 27335115](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27335115/), [www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK1305/)

**Disposition:** Penetrance qualified; intended inheritance key retained. **Access:** Accessible primary/official page or PubMed abstract; not a claim of full-text access.

### Kaplan

**2.32** — Autism spectrum disorder spans a wide range of intellectual and adaptive abilities. An IQ below 34 is not a general defining or usual level for all autistic children. The book percentage and IQ-based severity bands reflect its selected source-era framing. CDC surveillance also shows that intellectual disability is present in a subset, not all, of children with ASD; estimates depend on age, ascertainment and availability of cognitive data. Contemporary intellectual-disability severity is based on adaptive functioning. The source key cannot be generalized to every autistic child. [www.cdc.gov](https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/74/ss/ss7402a1.htm), [www.psychiatry.org](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/intellectual-disability/expert-q-and-a)

**Disposition:** Historical population generalization qualified. **Access:** Accessible primary/official page or PubMed abstract; not a claim of full-text access.

**3.2** — An acute fluctuating cognitive/behavioral change suggests delirium superimposed on prior dementia and warrants a search for a cause. The book proposes a urinary infection, but metronidazole has no clinically relevant activity against facultative anaerobes or obligate aerobes and is not an adequate default treatment for an ordinary aerobic bacterial UTI. The printed choices do not establish an appropriate replacement antibiotic. Infection and antibiotic selection require the actual assessment and microbiology; retain C only as the printed key, not as an endorsed prescription. [dailymed.nlm.nih.gov](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=e313c7d9-fb7b-4d0f-bccf-cc72ac5ef205)

**Disposition:** Printed treatment key not endorsed; replacement not inferred. **Access:** Accessible primary/official page or PubMed abstract; not a claim of full-text access.

**4.11** — Pellagra is caused by niacin (vitamin B3) deficiency, whereas thiamine (B1) deficiency causes Wernicke encephalopathy. Nutritional deficiencies can coexist, and pellagra encephalopathy may be overlooked in malnourished patients. The original explanation incorrectly equates pellagra with thiamine deficiency. Niacin is not among the printed choices, and the abbreviated presentation does not establish a unique corrected vitamin answer. Do not silently relabel the syndrome or replace the key by guesswork. [ods.od.nih.gov](https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/Niacin-HealthProfessional/), [PubMed 3401685](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3401685/)

**Disposition:** Confirmed explanatory error; options remain incomplete. **Access:** Accessible primary/official page or PubMed abstract; not a claim of full-text access.

**6.1** — Mania usually requires at least one week, or any duration if hospitalization is necessary; hypomania requires at least four consecutive days. Hypomania does not cause the marked impairment or need for hospitalization that defines mania-level severity. Therefore both episode duration and hospitalization/severity are relevant distinctions. The book explanation denying a duration difference is incorrect, so its single-choice framing does not cleanly distinguish A from keyed E. No new unique key is inferred. [www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519704/), [www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519704/table/ch3.t9/)

**Disposition:** Multiple relevant distinctions; source duration claim corrected. **Access:** Official criteria reproduction, retrieved indexed passages; full page access limited.

**13.5** — A binge-eating episode involves an objectively unusually large amount of food for the circumstances in a discrete period, together with loss of control. There is no fixed 2,000-kcal diagnostic cutoff. Keyed D approximates size/time but does not supply loss of control. Episode definition must also be distinguished from the frequency, duration, distress and other criteria for binge-eating disorder. The original numerical threshold and conflation of an episode with the full disorder should not be taught as diagnostic rules. [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10592441/)

**Disposition:** Fixed calorie cutoff corrected; choice incomplete. **Access:** Primary study indexed passages; PMC full page blocked by browser challenge.

**21.46** — Valproate carries important fetal malformation and neurodevelopmental risks. Folic acid is recommended but has not been shown to eliminate valproate-related risk. In this severely recurrent bipolar case with failed alternatives, continuing valproate is not automatically an incorrect choice: the label permits use when alternatives have failed or are unacceptable. B is therefore conditional, not a universally safe pregnancy plan. Specialist preconception risk-benefit review, alternatives and informed planning are required; do not stop abruptly. A serum concentration of 50 micrograms/mL is not a pregnancy-safety threshold. Epilepsy pregnancy guidance informs drug risk but does not by itself decide treatment of refractory bipolar disorder. [dailymed.nlm.nih.gov](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=319bbead-bec8-4bb7-9ecb-b2e161c44b8d), [PubMed 38748979](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38748979/)

**Disposition:** Conditional key; folate does not neutralize drug risk. **Access:** Accessible primary/official page or PubMed abstract; not a claim of full-text access.

**25.1** — Psychosomatic medicine was renamed consultation-liaison psychiatry as an ABPN subspecialty effective January 1, 2018; the professional academy also changed its name in 2018. This is a specialty/organization terminology history, not evidence that DSM deleted a diagnosis. The question conflates those contexts. Preserve the intended historical name association while explicitly separating it from DSM diagnostic categories. [abpn.org](https://abpn.org/become-certified/taking-a-subspecialty-exam/psychosomatic-medicine/), [clpsychiatry.org](https://clpsychiatry.org/about-aclp/aclp-name/)

**Disposition:** Specialty naming distinguished from diagnosis. **Access:** Accessible primary/official page or PubMed abstract; not a claim of full-text access.

**25.22** — Lithium toxicity with seizures or decreased consciousness requires urgent monitored hospital care. EXTRIP recommends extracorporeal treatment in these severe manifestations regardless of the serum lithium concentration; stop lithium, assess renal function/electrolytes and provide appropriate isotonic volume resuscitation. Routine osmotic diuresis is not the recommended elimination strategy, and activated charcoal does not bind lithium. Keyed E is not a complete appropriate plan; the outpatient dialysis choice is also inappropriate for this presentation. EXTRIP recommendations are consensus-based with very low-quality evidence, not randomized-trial proof. This does not change the correctly identified culprit in 25.21. [PubMed 25583292](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25583292/), [pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4422246/)

**Disposition:** Unsafe/incomplete source management qualified; no replacement key. **Access:** Accessible primary/official page or PubMed abstract; not a claim of full-text access.

**25.27** — Early CNS involvement in pediatric HIV does not establish that the brain is universally the primary site or that CSF becomes positive before blood in every infant. Current NIH infant-diagnosis guidance uses virologic nucleic-acid testing of blood/plasma, rather than a universal CSF-first rule. The supplied evidence does not validate the book generalization or establish a unique replacement primary-site answer. Distinguish early tissue involvement from a clinically recommended diagnostic specimen. [clinicalinfo.hiv.gov](https://clinicalinfo.hiv.gov/en/guidelines/pediatric-arv/diagnosis-hiv-infection-infants-and-children)

**Disposition:** Unsupported universal site/timing claim; no substitute asserted. **Access:** Accessible primary/official page or PubMed abstract; not a claim of full-text access.

**28.14** — The historical M’Naghten formulation concerns a defect of reason due to disease of mind that prevents knowing the nature/quality of the act, or knowing that it was wrong. A delusional self-defense example is not a necessary fact in every successful insanity defense. A diagnosis or delusion alone is also insufficient. The book key should not be taught as a universal necessary condition. Insanity standards vary by jurisdiction; this is a historical rule clarification, not verification of current local law. [www.bailii.org](https://www.bailii.org/uk/cases/UKHL/1843/J16.html), [PubMed 25492121](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25492121/)

**Disposition:** Alleged necessary condition qualified; jurisdiction unresolved. **Access:** Primary judgment indexed passage and PubMed guideline record; judgment/full guideline access blocked.

**29.9** — Endicott substitution addresses somatic confounding in depression assessment during physical illness by replacing selected appetite, sleep, fatigue and concentration symptoms with psychological/behavioral indicators. Appetite change is a symptom being replaced, not itself a replacement marker. Thus keyed B is problematic if the question asks for a substitute indicator. If part of the criteria means the wider assessment rather than the substitute list, the wording is ambiguous. The printed options do not support guessing a new unique key. [PubMed 6704912](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6704912/), [PubMed 19410101](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19410101/)

**Disposition:** Substituted symptoms distinguished from substitute indicators. **Access:** Original PubMed abstract plus indexed primary validation-study abstract; not full articles.

**30.3** — Universal prevention addresses everyone; selective prevention addresses a higher-risk group without individual early symptoms; indicated prevention addresses individuals with early or subthreshold signs before a full disorder develops. Keyed selective prevention is defensible if the adolescents truly have no symptoms and are identified only by risk. A high symptom-screen score may instead reflect subthreshold symptoms, making indicated prevention appropriate. The stated high score/no-symptoms combination therefore needs clarification. Treatment of an established depressive disorder is not simply indicated prevention. [www.samhsa.gov](https://www.samhsa.gov/sites/default/files/fy24-25-subg-report.pdf)

**Disposition:** Conditional selective key retained; symptom status ambiguous. **Access:** Official prevention definitions retrieved through indexed passages.

**32.8** — CDC lists smiling in response to interaction among milestones by two months; this is not a claim that every infant first smiles at exactly that age. The printed four-month key conflicts with the book explanation saying four weeks. Neither an exact four-week universal onset nor an automatic replacement by the one-month choice is supported by this check. Preserve the discrepancy explicitly and distinguish developmental variability from a diagnostic conclusion. [www.cdc.gov](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/2-months.html)

**Disposition:** Printed timing conflict; no exact new onset/key inferred. **Access:** Accessible primary/official page or PubMed abstract; not a claim of full-text access.

**33.16** — The dopaminergic projection from the ventral tegmental area to the nucleus accumbens participates in reward and reinforcement. The mesoaccumbens projection is part of, or overlaps with, what is commonly called the mesolimbic reward circuit; these labels are not mutually exclusive alternatives. Keyed C identifies the more specific projection, but A cannot be rejected merely by claiming that mesolimbic signaling only concerns schizophrenia. This terminology overlap prevents a clean unique distinction. [PubMed 28396627](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28396627/), [PubMed 17574681](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17574681/)

**Disposition:** Overlapping pathway labels; unique grading not established. **Access:** Accessible primary/official page or PubMed abstract; not a claim of full-text access.

**33.24** — A relative risk of 1.2 means occurrence is 1.2 times that in the reference group: a 20% relative increase. It does not mean a 20% absolute risk, an additional 20 percentage points, or a 20% probability that genes caused the disease. For example, a 10% reference risk would become 12%, not 30%. Familial aggregation alone does not separate genetic from shared environmental effects. The printed answer choices do not correctly express this interpretation, so B remains source provenance rather than a valid statistical inference. [www.cdc.gov](https://www.cdc.gov/field-epi-manual/php/chapters/analyze-interpret-data.html)

**Disposition:** Relative risk corrected; no accurate printed option. **Access:** Accessible primary/official page or PubMed abstract; not a claim of full-text access.
